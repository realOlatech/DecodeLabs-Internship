# E-Commerce Sales & Order Performance Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes an e-commerce order dataset containing **1,200 orders** to understand sales performance, order fulfillment, customer purchasing behavior, payment methods, and marketing/referral performance.

The project combines **Excel data analysis** and **SQL analysis** to transform raw transactional data into business insights that can support decision-making around sales, operations, customer engagement, and marketing.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

An e-commerce business needs to understand how its orders and revenue are performing across different products, order statuses, payment methods, customers, and referral sources.

The key business questions addressed in this project include:

- Which products generate the most revenue?
- How many orders are delivered, shipped, pending, cancelled, or returned?
- Which payment methods generate the most revenue?
- Which referral sources generate the most orders and revenue?
- Which customers contribute the most revenue?
- How much revenue is associated with delivered orders?
- What operational areas may require further attention?

---

# 🔎 Analysis Performed

## 1. Product Revenue Analysis

I analyzed revenue generated by each product for delivered orders.

| Product | Revenue |
|---|---:|
| Laptop | $40,714.43 |
| Phone | $40,345.41 |
| Printer | $38,054.73 |
| Monitor | $35,999.62 |
| Tablet | $31,794.52 |
| Chair | $31,465.83 |
| Desk | $24,225.78 |

### Insight

Among delivered orders, **Laptops generated the highest revenue at $40,714.43**, followed closely by Phones at $40,345.41.

Desks generated the lowest revenue among the analyzed products at $24,225.78.

### Business Recommendation

The business should monitor inventory availability and sales performance for high-revenue products such as laptops, phones, and printers.

Lower-revenue products should be investigated further to determine whether the difference is related to demand, pricing, quantity sold, product positioning, or other factors.

# 2. Order Status Analysis

The dataset contains the following order statuses:

| Order Status | Orders | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cancelled | 250 | 20.8% |
| Returned | 247 | 20.6% |
| Pending | 237 | 19.8% |
| Shipped | 235 | 19.6% |
| Delivered | 231 | 19.2% |

### Insight

The dataset shows a relatively even distribution across the five order statuses.

Only **231 of the 1,200 orders (19.2%)** are recorded as delivered, while cancelled, returned, pending, and shipped orders collectively account for the remaining orders.

### Business Recommendation

The business should investigate the reasons behind cancelled and returned orders and identify bottlenecks contributing to pending orders.

A deeper operational analysis could examine:

- Cancellation reasons
- Return reasons
- Delivery times
- Shipping performance
- Product-level return rates
- Customer-level order behavior

This would help determine where operational improvements could have the greatest impact.

# 4. Referral Source Analysis

For delivered orders:

| Referral Source | Orders | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| Email | 60 | $64,553.52 |
| Facebook | 42 | $52,149.38 |
| Instagram | 52 | $51,299.56 |
| Google | 44 | $41,406.25 |
| Referral | 33 | $33,191.61 |

### Insight

**Email generated the highest delivered-order revenue ($64,553.52) and the highest number of delivered orders (60).**

Instagram generated 52 delivered orders, while Facebook generated 42.

The Referral channel generated the fewest delivered orders and the lowest delivered-order revenue among the listed sources.

### Business Recommendation

The company should measure marketing channels using both **order volume and revenue**, rather than relying on traffic or order count alone.

Email campaigns could be analyzed further to identify which campaigns, customer segments, or offers are responsible for higher-value purchases.

Other channels should also be evaluated based on customer acquisition cost and return on marketing investment before increasing or reducing spending.

# 🧮 SQL Analysis

I used SQL Server to consolidate the different analytical views into a single result using **Common Table Expressions (CTEs)** and `UNION ALL`.

The analysis included:

- Product revenue
- Order status
- Payment method revenue
- Customer revenue
- Referral source performance

Example SQL structure:

```sql
WITH ProductAnalysis AS
(
    SELECT
        'Product' AS AnalysisType,
        Product AS Category,
        NULL AS OrderCount,
        SUM(TotalPrice) AS Revenue
    FROM [dbo].[Dataset for Data Analytics (1)]
    WHERE OrderStatus = 'Delivered'
    GROUP BY Product
),

OrderStatusAnalysis AS
(
    SELECT
        'Order Status' AS AnalysisType,
        OrderStatus AS Category,
        COUNT(OrderID) AS OrderCount,
        SUM(TotalPrice) AS Revenue
    FROM [dbo].[Dataset for Data Analytics (1)]
    GROUP BY OrderStatus
)

-- Additional analytical CTEs
-- Product
-- Payment Method
-- Customer
-- Referral Source

SELECT *
FROM ProductAnalysis

UNION ALL

SELECT *
FROM OrderStatusAnalysis;
